## Key Findings

### Central Bank Liquidity and Oversight

Regulators consider central bank purchases as a tool to address fire sale dynamics in stablecoin markets, particularly given the U.S. dominance in the stablecoin space and the fact that stablecoin deposits are less efficient as bank funding sources, with the design of stablecoins—especially their reserve composition and access to central bank accounts—shaping their classification as money-like or securities-like. [[1]](https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/087/2025/009/article-A001-en.xml)[[2]](https://www.sec.gov/files/stablecoin_regulatory_framework.pdf)[[8]](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/pub/pdf/scpops/ecb.op353~11120d3428.en.pdf)[[10]](https://www.bis.org/publications/aer-2026/anchoring-trust-money)

As regulatory frameworks for stablecoins evolve, greater clarity on eligibility for central bank liquidity and oversight will emerge. [[1]](https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/087/2025/009/article-A001-en.xml) Operational preparedness of central banks for such circumstances is a key precondition for financial stability. [[1]](https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/087/2025/009/article-A001-en.xml) The second expectation relates to the use of stablecoins as money and stated that systemic stablecoins should meet standards equivalent to those provided by commercial bank money in relation to stability of value, robustness of legal claim and the ability to redeem at par in fiat. [[11]](https://www.fsb.org/uploads/P071021.pdf)

The dynamics between stablecoin issuers and credit institutions explore how deposits from stablecoin issuers affect banks’ liquidity and capital ratios and thereby influence their risk profile. [[8]](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/pub/pdf/scpops/ecb.op353~11120d3428.en.pdf) The GENIUS Act creates a clearly defined legal category of Permitted Payment Stablecoin Issuers (PPSIs). [[12]](https://regulatorystudies.columbian.gwu.edu/rise-and-regulation-payment-stablecoins) Payment stablecoins must be backed on a one-for-one basis by high-quality, highly liquid reserve assets such as cash, deposits at insured depository institutions, balances at Federal Reserve Banks, and short-term U.S. Treasury securities. [[12]](https://regulatorystudies.columbian.gwu.edu/rise-and-regulation-payment-stablecoins)

Large stablecoin redemptions could negatively impact money markets and funding conditions. [[10]](https://www.bis.org/publications/aer-2026/anchoring-trust-money) Qualifying outflow rates for stablecoin liabilities when the bank is the stablecoin issuer are addressed in the paper. [[8]](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/pub/pdf/scpops/ecb.op353~11120d3428.en.pdf)

### Regulatory Frameworks and Jurisdictional Approaches

Issuers must ensure all holders have a legally enforceable right to redeem their funds on demand without unreasonable conditions. [[9]](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/paper/2026/boe-and-fcas-approach-to-joint-regulation-of-systemic-stablecoin-issuers) The Bank requires issuers to complete redemptions within 24 hours and in real time wherever possible. [[9]](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/paper/2026/boe-and-fcas-approach-to-joint-regulation-of-systemic-stablecoin-issuers) A firm must hold at least 10% of the value of its backing asset pool in core backing assets. [[9]](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/paper/2026/boe-and-fcas-approach-to-joint-regulation-of-systemic-stablecoin-issuers)

Different jurisdictions have different policies toward stablecoins. [[13]](https://www.bis.org/publications/considerations-use-stablecoin-arrangements-cross-border-payments) Some jurisdictions refuse to accept stablecoins due to risks to monetary sovereignty, financial stability, and seigniorage income. [[13]](https://www.bis.org/publications/considerations-use-stablecoin-arrangements-cross-border-payments) Other jurisdictions regulate stablecoins to manage risks and recognize their potential role in future payment ecosystems. [[13]](https://www.bis.org/publications/considerations-use-stablecoin-arrangements-cross-border-payments)

A legal framework for the issuance of payment stablecoins is established, modeled after the dual-banking system. [[3]](https://democrats-financialservices.house.gov/uploadedfiles/02.10.2025_s_b_s_stablecoinb.pdf) Depository institution stablecoin issuers would be regulated by their primary Federal banking regulator. [[3]](https://democrats-financialservices.house.gov/uploadedfiles/02.10.2025_s_b_s_stablecoinb.pdf) Nonbank stablecoin issuers would be licensed either with the Fed or a State with some Fed oversight. [[3]](https://democrats-financialservices.house.gov/uploadedfiles/02.10.2025_s_b_s_stablecoinb.pdf)

Regulatory requirements for stablecoin issuers are specified in section 3.3 of the report. [[7]](https://www.fsb.org/uploads/P161025-1.pdf) The Guiding and Establishing National Innovation for U.S. Stablecoins Act seeks to establish a formal framework for stablecoin issuance and oversight in the US. [[15]](https://www.visa.com/en-us/thought-leadership/global-money-movement/stablecoins-are-reinventing-cross-border-payments) Policymakers should avoid building isolated rules that trap stablecoins inside national borders. [[16]](https://nhimg.org/faq/how-should-policymakers-balance-stablecoin-innovation-with-cross-border-regulato)

Payment stablecoins present a distinct set of cross-border challenges. [[17]](https://academic.oup.com/cmlj/article/21/2/kmag015/8665079)

### Cross-border Coordination and Financial Stability

The ECB's internal crypto-assets task force focuses on assessing and identifying how to contain adverse impacts of crypto-assets on the use of the euro, monetary policy, market infrastructure, payments, and financial stability. [[4]](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/pub/pdf/scpops/ecb.op247~fe3df92991.en.pdf) A risk assessment for stablecoins currently in operation does not significantly differ from the current ECB stance on crypto-assets, but could change substantially with new stablecoin initiatives. [[4]](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/pub/pdf/scpops/ecb.op247~fe3df92991.en.pdf) MiCA sets out a comprehensive regulatory framework for all crypto-assets not already covered under other European financial services legislation, including stablecoins. [[11]](https://www.fsb.org/uploads/P071021.pdf)

The regulatory framework should be comprehensive, adequately covering all entities carrying out core functions, including issuers and crypto asset service providers that interact with the stablecoin, such wallets, exchanges, and reserve managers. [[6]](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/ftn063/2022/english/ftnea2022008.pdf) Any global regulatory framework for stablecoins should be comprehensive, risk-based, and flexible, and it should provide a level playing field. [[6]](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/ftn063/2022/english/ftnea2022008.pdf) Comprehensive regulatory and supervisory oversight of stablecoins can help to address financial stability and financial integrity risks. [[5]](https://www.fsb.org/uploads/P230724.pdf)

Demand for foreign stablecoins in emerging market and developing economies could reshape capital flows, affect exchange rate dynamics and challenge monetary sovereignty. [[10]](https://www.bis.org/publications/aer-2026/anchoring-trust-money) The policy objective is not regulatory sameness, it is regulatory compatibility. [[16]](https://nhimg.org/faq/how-should-policymakers-balance-stablecoin-innovation-with-cross-border-regulato) Central banks in China, the EU and elsewhere are developing CBDCs that interact with and compete with commercial stablecoins, making their interplay a closely watched dynamic in global payments. [[15]](https://www.visa.com/en-us/thought-leadership/global-money-movement/stablecoins-are-reinventing-cross-border-payments)
